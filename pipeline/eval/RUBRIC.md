# Relevance rubric for matcher evaluation

Each item is a pair: an applicant's description of their research interests (the query) and one faculty member's
public research profile. Grade how good a PhD-advisor fit the faculty member is for this applicant, **using only the
profile shown** (summary, keywords, research areas, labels, profile excerpt). Do not use outside knowledge of the person.

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 3 | Excellent fit. Their documented research directly addresses the applicant's core topic **and** matches its specific context or approach (same phenomenon, population, region, platform or method). A natural first choice. |
| 2 | Good fit. Substantial overlap on the applicant's core topic; a credible advisor even if the context or method differs. |
| 1 | Partial. Overlap only on a secondary aspect (the same method, region or population with a different topic), or a broad area that merely contains the topic. |
| 0 | Not relevant, or overlap only on generic words (for example both mention "social media" or "communication" but study different things). |

Guidance
- Judge the core research question first; methods and regions refine the grade, they do not create relevance on their own.
- Short keyword queries (e.g. "data journalism") have no secondary aspects: 3 = the topic is central to their work, 2 = a clear secondary strand, 1 = touches it, 0 = no.
- Chinese-language queries: read the Chinese; grade exactly as for English.
- Practitioners (filmmakers, journalists) can be relevant when their creative or professional work is on the topic.
- Be consistent across candidates of the same query; grade each pair independently of the others' grades.
